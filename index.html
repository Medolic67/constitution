Article I · Legislative Branch

Congress makes the laws

Congress is bicameral (House + Senate) and holds enumerated powers plus the Necessary and Proper Clause. It is the longest article, because the framers saw the legislature as the central branch.

House · 2-yr terms · by population Senate · 6-yr terms · 2 per state 10 sections
POWER what the branch can do (incl. privileges) LIMIT what it can't do, or a restriction on a power RULE structure, terms & qualifications
Sec1

Legislative power

  • All legislative powers granted go to Congress bicameralism
Sec2

House of Representatives

  • Elected directly by the people every 2 years
  • Qualifications: 25 years old · 7 years a citizen · lives in the state
  • Seats apportioned by population · census every 10 years · every state gets at least one
  • Governor calls special elections to fill vacancies
  • Chooses its Speaker and other officers
  • Sole power of impeachment (bringing charges)
Sec3

Senate

  • Two senators per state, 6-year terms, one vote each
  • Staggered: one-third elected every 2 years
  • Qualifications: 30 years old · 9 years a citizen · lives in the state
  • Vice President presides but votes only to break a tie
  • Chooses its officers, including a President pro tempore
  • Sole power to try impeachments (Chief Justice presides when the President is tried)
  • Conviction requires 2/3 of members present
  • Punishment is limited to removal and disqualification from office (a criminal trial is still possible)
Sec4

Elections & meetings

  • States set the times, places, and manner of congressional elections, and Congress may change them
  • Congress must meet at least once a year
Sec5

Rules of each house

  • Each house judges its own members' elections and qualifications
  • Each house sets its own rules and punishes members for disorderly behavior
  • Expelling a member requires 2/3
  • A majority is a quorum
  • Each keeps a published journal; 1/5 of members can demand a recorded vote
  • Neither house may adjourn for more than 3 days without the other's consent
Sec6

Privileges & restrictions

  • Paid from the Treasury by law (27th Am.: a pay raise waits until after the next election)
  • Free from arrest while going to or attending sessions, except for treason, felony, or breach of the peace
  • Can't be questioned elsewhere for what they say in Congress Speech or Debate
  • Can't take a federal office created, or given a raise, during their term
  • Can't hold another federal office while serving separation of powers
Sec7

How a bill becomes law

  • Revenue bills must start in the House; the Senate may amend them
  • Every bill must go to the President, who can sign it or veto it
  • Congress can override a veto with 2/3 of both houses
  • No action in 10 days (Sundays excepted) → law, unless Congress has adjourned, in which case it dies pocket veto

→ See the full flowchart in Powers & Processes.

Sec8

Enumerated powers of Congress

💰 Money & economy

  • Tax for debts, defense, general welfare
  • Borrow money
  • Regulate foreign, interstate & tribal commerce Commerce Clause
  • Coin money; weights & measures
  • Punish counterfeiting
  • Bankruptcy laws

⚔ Defense & war

  • Declare war; letters of marque
  • Raise and support armies
  • Maintain a navy
  • Make military rules
  • Call forth & organize the militia
  • Punish piracy

🏛 Government

  • Naturalization rules
  • Post offices & roads
  • Copyrights & patents
  • Create lower federal courts
  • Govern D.C. & federal property

🧩 Elastic clause

  • Make all laws necessary and proper to carry out these powers implied powers
  • Upheld in McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)

⛓ Built-in limits

  • Taxes, duties & excises must be uniform nationwide
  • Army funding can't run longer than 2 years
  • States appoint militia officers and do the training
  • The D.C. district can't exceed 10 miles square
Sec9

Limits on Congress

  • Habeas corpus can't be suspended except in rebellion or invasion
  • No bill of attainder (punishment without trial) or ex post facto law (retroactive crime)
  • Direct taxes are apportioned by population (16th Am. exempts income tax)
  • No taxes on exports; no favoring one state's ports
  • No spending without an appropriation by law; accounts must be published power of the purse
  • No titles of nobility; no foreign gifts or titles for officials without Congress's consent Emoluments
Sec10

Limits on the states

Never allowed

  • Treaties or alliances
  • Coin money or issue paper currency
  • Bills of attainder / ex post facto laws
  • Impair contracts
  • Titles of nobility

Only with Congress's consent

  • Tax imports or exports
  • Keep troops or warships in peacetime
  • Compacts with other states or nations
  • Wage war, unless invaded
Article II · Executive Branch

The President enforces the laws

The President is chosen through the Electoral College, and shares key powers (treaties, appointments) with the Senate.

4-year term 35+ years old Natural-born citizen 14 yrs resident
POWER what the branch can do (incl. duties) LIMIT what it can't do, or a restriction on a power RULE structure, terms & qualifications
Sec1

The office & election

  • Executive power is vested in the President
  • Elected with a VP for a 4-year term
  • Each state's electors = its senators + representatives, chosen as its legislature directs Electoral College
  • Members of Congress and federal officers can't serve as electors
  • Congress sets when electors are chosen and one national voting day
  • Qualifications: natural-born citizen · 35 · 14 years a resident
  • Congress may set the line of succession beyond the VP (see the 25th Am.)
  • Salary can't be raised or cut during the term, and no other federal or state payments are allowed
  • Oath: "preserve, protect and defend the Constitution"
Sec2

Powers & their limits

✅ Powers

  • Commander in Chief of the armed forces and the federalized militia
  • Can require written opinions from department heads (origin of the Cabinet)
  • Grants pardons & reprieves for federal crimes
  • Makes treaties
  • Nominates ambassadors, Supreme Court justices & officers
  • Makes recess appointments while the Senate is out

⛔ Limits

  • Commands the militia only when it's called into federal service
  • Pardons can't cover impeachment or state crimes
  • Treaties need 2/3 of the Senate
  • Appointments need Senate advice & consent (majority)
  • Recess appointments expire at the end of the next session
  • Congress decides who appoints "inferior officers"
Sec3

Duties

  • Gives a State of the Union report and recommends laws
  • Can convene Congress in emergencies, and adjourn it if the houses disagree
  • Receives ambassadors (recognizes foreign governments)
  • "Take care that the laws be faithfully executed" Take Care Clause
  • Commissions all federal officers
Sec4

Impeachment

  • The President, VP, and civil officers are removed on conviction for treason, bribery, or other high crimes and misdemeanors
Article III · Judicial Branch

The courts interpret the laws

Only the Supreme Court is required; Congress creates the lower courts. Judges serve during "good behaviour." Judicial review isn't written here. It comes from Marbury v. Madison (1803).

Life tenure Pay can't be cut Only crime defined: treason
POWER what the courts can do (incl. protections) LIMIT what they can't do, or a restriction on a power RULE structure, terms & qualifications
Sec1

Federal courts

  • Judicial power is vested in one Supreme Court
  • Lower courts exist only as Congress creates them
  • Judges serve during good behaviour life tenure
  • Salaries can't be reduced while in office judicial independence
Sec2

Jurisdiction & jury trials

  • Federal courts hear cases under the Constitution, federal laws, and treaties; ambassador cases; maritime cases; cases where the U.S. is a party; disputes between states; between citizens of different states diversity; and cases with foreign nations

Original jurisdiction

  • The Supreme Court hears these first
  • Cases with ambassadors
  • Cases where a state is a party

Appellate jurisdiction

  • Hears all other cases on appeal, both law and fact

Limits

  • Congress can make exceptions to appellate jurisdiction
  • Courts hear only actual "cases and controversies"
  • Federal crimes (except impeachment) must be tried by jury in the state where they happened
Sec3

Treason

  • Treason is only levying war against the U.S., or adhering to its enemies by giving them aid and comfort
  • Conviction requires 2 witnesses to the same overt act or a confession in open court
  • Congress sets the punishment for treason
  • The punishment can't extend to the traitor's family no corruption of blood
Article IV · Federalism

How the states relate

This article covers how states treat each other and what the national government owes them.

Sec1

Full faith and credit

  • States honor each other's public acts, records, and court rulings (e.g., marriages, licenses, judgments)
Sec2

Interstate obligations

  • Citizens get the privileges and immunities of citizens in every state; states can't discriminate against out-of-staters
  • Fugitives charged with crimes must be returned on demand extradition
Sec3

New states & territories

  • Congress admits new states; none can be carved from or merged with existing states without their consent
  • Congress governs federal territory and property
Sec4

Guarantee clause

  • Every state is guaranteed a republican form of government
  • Federal protection from invasion, and from domestic violence when the state requests it
Article V · Amendment

Changing the Constitution

Amendments are possible but hard, because every route requires supermajorities. There are two ways to propose and two ways to ratify.

Step 1 · Propose

Option A — used for all 272/3 of the House and 2/3 of the Senate
— OR —
Option B — never used2/3 of state legislatures ask Congress to call a national convention

Step 2 · Ratify (Congress picks the method)

Option A — used 26 times3/4 of state legislatures (38 of 50)
— OR —
Option B — used once (21st Am.)Ratifying conventions in 3/4 of states

🔒 Permanent protection: no state can lose its equal vote in the Senate without its own consent.
Note that the President plays no formal role in amendments: no signature and no veto.

Article VI

Supreme law of the land

Federal law beats conflicting state law.

¶1

Prior debts

  • Debts made under the Articles of Confederation stay valid
¶2

Supremacy Clause

  • The Constitution, federal laws made under it, and treaties are the supreme law of the land
  • State judges are bound by them, even over their own state's laws
¶3

Oaths

  • All federal and state officials swear to support the Constitution
  • No religious test may ever be required for office

The ratification debate

Federalists

  • Hamilton, Madison, Jay
  • The Federalist Papers (e.g., Fed 10, 51)
  • Wanted a stronger national government

Anti-Federalists

  • e.g., Brutus, Federal Farmer
  • Feared a distant, powerful government
  • Demanded a bill of rights
Amendments I–X · 1791

Bill of Rights

The Bill of Rights was added to win over the Anti-Federalists. It originally limited only the federal government. Most of it now applies to the states through the 14th Amendment incorporation.

I–III · Freedoms IV–VIII · Justice & the accused IX–X · Unlisted rights & powers

Expression & religion

  • No established religion · free exercise
  • Speech & press
  • Assemble & petition
II

Bear arms

  • Militia being necessary, the right to keep and bear arms shall not be infringed
III

No quartering

  • No soldiers in homes in peacetime without consent
  • In wartime only as law allows
IV

Search & seizure

  • No unreasonable searches or seizures
  • Warrants need probable cause, an oath, and specifics
V

Rights of the accused

  • Grand jury for serious crimes
  • No double jeopardy
  • No self-incrimination
  • Due process
  • Just compensation eminent domain
VI

Criminal trials

  • Speedy, public trial; impartial local jury
  • Know the charges; confront witnesses
  • Compel favorable witnesses
  • Right to a lawyer
VII

Civil juries

  • Jury in common-law suits over $20
  • Jury's facts not re-examined except by common-law rules
VIII

Punishment

  • No excessive bail or fines
  • No cruel and unusual punishment
IX

Unlisted rights

  • Listing some rights doesn't deny others the people keep
X

Reserved powers

  • Powers not delegated to the U.S. nor denied to states belong to the states or the people federalism

Checks & balances

Each branch can check the other two.

Congress checks…

→ the President
  • Override vetoes (2/3) I §7
  • Impeach & remove I §2–3
  • Senate rejects treaties & appointees II §2
  • Controls funding; declares war I §8–9
→ the Courts
  • Senate confirms judges II §2
  • Impeach judges I §2–3
  • Creates lower courts; sets appellate exceptions III §1–2
  • Proposes amendments V

The President checks…

→ Congress
  • Veto I §7
  • Call special sessions II §3
  • Recommend legislation (State of the Union) II §3
→ the Courts
  • Nominates judges II §2
  • Pardons II §2
  • Enforces rulings II §3

The Courts check…

→ Congress
  • Strike down unconstitutional laws III §2 · Marbury
→ the President
  • Strike down unconstitutional executive actions III §2
  • Chief Justice presides over presidential impeachment trials I §3
